One question, three answers

There is no such thing as the price of a flight.

There is the price for somebody. A budget carrier sells the seat and charges for the rest. A full service carrier sells the bag inside the fare. Sorting on the headline answers a question nobody asked.

Every figure here is read out of the recorded search, not typed in.

Six ways this goes wrong

  1. One deadline, not eight timeouts

    The budget is set once, at the edge. Every call below gets what is left of it, never a fresh allowance.

  2. A partial answer beats none

    A supplier that misses the deadline is reported beside the results. Failing the search because one of eight was slow is the expensive mistake.

  3. Late is not broken

    A dead supplier stops being called. A slow one keeps being called, because counting slowness as failure drops suppliers that work.

  4. Normalisation hides the bugs

    Per passenger or per booking, different currencies, local time with no offset. Comparing the numbers as they arrive compares different things.

  5. One flight, one row

    The same seats sold by three suppliers is one row with three prices. Three rows look full and hide the spread.

  6. A lapsed fare is worse than none

    It sorts to the top, gets clicked, and fails where somebody was about to pay. So it is dropped, and counted.

The boundary

The budget survives the hop, or it is not a budget.

Two services, and the deadline crosses between them in a header. A budget that stops at a process boundary is a timeout with extra steps, and the service below quietly starts a fresh one.

GET /suppliers/fineair/fares?origin=DUS&destination=STN
X-Fanout-Deadline-Ms: 842
  • Shorten it, never extend it. The service starts at its own ceiling and takes whichever is sooner. Ask for ten minutes, get five seconds.
  • 504 and 502 are different answers. Out of budget against actually broken. The breaker counts one and ignores the other.
  • Money crosses as minor units. Never a decimal. Read back as a double, 21.49 times 100 is 2148.9999.

What is real here

The airlines are invented

Fineair, Bizzair, Altair and Halcyon do not exist. There is no free flight-price API left to record from, so putting invented prices beside a real brand was not worth doing.

The timings are measured

Every millisecond on this page came from a real search across two processes. Nothing here is rounded to look tidy, and no number was typed in by hand.
